GRADE recommendations

PG 70-22

SBS polymer-modified bitumen with resistance to rutting, fatigue and low-temperature cracking.

  1. 1. For heavy traffic in hot climates, consider PG 70-22 and confirm the project requirements.
  2. 2. For hot, humid coastal conditions. Confirm the pavement design temperatures for your site.
  3. 3. For a contractor: take this into the specification meeting, then match it to the project clause before you order.

How a road is built

Six layers. One road.

  1. Existing laterite track, Before construction: The red-earth road as it is today: dust in the dry season, ruts and standing water in the rains.
  2. Compacted sub-base, Crushed aggregate: Crushed stone is spread and rolled flat. It carries the load and lets water drain away.
  3. Prime coat, Project-specified primer: Applied to the stone base, it binds loose particles and prepares the surface for the asphalt layer.
  4. Binder course, AC-20 hot mix: Hot asphalt is laid and compacted. This is the structural layer that spreads each wheel load.
  5. Tack coat, K1-40 / K1-55 emulsion: A thin film bonds the asphalt layers, so the two courses work as one.
  6. Wearing course, SBS PMB PG 76-22: The surface traffic runs on. Polymer-modified binder resists rutting in tropical heat.
